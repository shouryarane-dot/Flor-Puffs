# FLŌR — Art Direction & Shoot Brief

For a photographer, a stylist, or a half-day you shoot yourselves.
Everything here feeds directly into the website rebuild; the shot list is mapped to page slots with aspect ratios.

---

## The world

Quiet luxury, botanical editorial, Mediterranean afternoon.
A pouch of makhana sitting on a sun-warmed stone ledge in a house on a Greek island, next to a glass of wine, with a lotus leaf throwing a shadow across the plaster.

**Reference register:** Aesop, Loewe Home Scents, Flamingo Estate, Jacquemus campaign stills, Sunday Riley. Fashion and fragrance, not food.

**The single organising idea: shadows are the subject.** Hard, low, late-afternoon light. One source, no fill, no diffusion. The product is often small in frame. What fills the frame is stone, light and shadow.

## Palette

Pull from the packs, not from a mood board.

| | Hex | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Brand brown | `#522F19` | the logo ink |
| Pack cream | `#E4DBC9` | the pouch face |
| Salt navy | `#1F4368` | Classic Salt |
| Peri terracotta | `#B94A36` | Peri Peri |
| Cocoa burgundy | `#6B2938` | Cocoa |

Everything photographic should sit in warm ivory / travertine / sand, so the three pack colours read as the only saturated notes in the whole brand.

Shadows must stay **warm** — brown-grey, never blue-grey. This is the difference between Mediterranean and clinical.

## Surfaces and props

**Buy or borrow:**

- Travertine or limestone offcut, roughly 40×40cm. Tile shops give offcuts away or sell them for a few euro. Unpolished, honed finish — not glossy.
- A board limewashed in warm ivory (Pure & Original sample pot, ~€10, painted onto MDF) for plaster walls.
- Raw, undyed linen. Crumpled, not pressed.
- Unglazed stoneware bowls, small. Matte.
- A shallow tray or dish for still water.
- **Lotus or water lily leaves and one flower** — Nienke's trade. This is the prop nobody else in the category can get easily, and it ties the photography to the actual ingredient rather than to generic "botanical".
- A glass of white wine, a linen napkin, one brass or bone-handled object.

**Banned props:** wooden chopping boards, hessian or burlap, wheat stalks, kraft paper, scattered "artful" crumbs, anything gym-adjacent, anything in a mason jar.

---

## Light

- Single hard source. Late afternoon sun through a window is ideal and free.
- Low angle, roughly 20–35° — long shadows, raking across texture.
- **No diffuser, no reflector, no fill.** Let shadows go deep.
- Shoot the same setup across 40 minutes as the sun moves; the best frame is usually not the one you planned.
- A lotus leaf or a slatted blind between the light and the wall gives the dappled botanical shadow that carries the whole identity.

If shooting on a phone: use RAW / ProRAW, lock exposure for the highlights, and **turn portrait mode off**. Fake bokeh is the single biggest giveaway of an amateur product shot.

## What to avoid

- Overhead flat-lays on white
- Symmetrical arrangements
- Soft, even, shadowless lighting
- Visible "wellness" signifiers — measuring tapes, dumbbells, green smoothies, superfood framing
- Cool or neutral white balance
- Anything rustic, farmhouse, or artisanal-craft coded
- The product floating with a drop shadow, which is what the site does now

---

## Where this goes next

Once shots 1, 3 and 4 exist, the site rebuild is straightforward:

- Hero becomes full-bleed photography with the wordmark set into the negative space, no cutout, no CSS shadow
- Flavour cards become photographs, not floating pouches on tinted washes
- Shot 6 replaces the lotus-line-art dividers
- Type stays as it is — the FLŌR serif is working; it's the imagery that's carrying the dated feeling
- Layout moves to asymmetric, full-bleed, dramatic crop, which only becomes possible once there are images worth cropping

**Budget reality:** travertine offcut plus limewash sample plus flowers is under €60. A half-day with a stills photographer in Amsterdam is roughly €400–800. Shooting it yourselves in an afternoon with a phone and good window light is genuinely viable for shots 1–4, and for a brand at your stage it's the right first move.
